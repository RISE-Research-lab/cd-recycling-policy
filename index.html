Hinkley Center funded research · 2026–2027

A Decision-Support Simulation Platform for Evaluating Construction and Demolition Debris Recycling Policies in Florida Counties

We are building a simulation tool that lets county officials test construction and demolition (C&D) debris recycling policies under their own local conditions, before they adopt them.

The challenge

Florida generates millions of tons of C&D debris every year, yet recycling performance varies widely from county to county. Most Florida counties operate without a diversion ordinance.

County officials who want to improve recycling have no practical way to see how a policy would perform locally before committing public resources. The options include diversion mandates, deposit-refund systems, differential tipping fees, facility certification, procurement mandates for recycled materials, or a combination.

What we are building

The platform models the key players in the C&D system: contractors of different sizes, recycling facilities, landfills, end-product markets, and regulators. It shows how they respond when policy conditions change.

The prototype is developed and tested for a single Florida county as a proof of concept, and is designed so county staff can enter their own local parameters.

Project schedule

Progress in the first quarter (August 16 to November 15, 2026)

  • County codes. We collected and read publicly available codes, ordinances, and fee schedules for Florida’s 67 counties. So far we found flow control rules in 36 counties, a dumping ban in 33, and a recycling provision in 6. This review is still under way, and a county’s category may change as we read more documents.
  • FDEP facility reports. We analyzed the annual C&D reports for 2021 to 2025. Reporting facilities handled 49.4 million tons over the five years, and about a third of those tons were handled outside their county of origin.
  • Literature review. We read 55 studies in full, along with seven published simulation models and nine Florida agency and Hinkley Center reports.
  • Conceptual model. We wrote the model specification, which the TAG will review at TAG Meeting 2.
  • Simulation model. We built a first version that runs on a synthetic test county. It is not yet calibrated to a Florida county, so it has produced no policy results.

These results are exploratory: they describe patterns in the records, not tested policy effects. Details are in the 1st Quarterly Progress Report (PDF).
