About the project

Project overview

Why counties need a way to test C&D recycling policies before adopting them, and how this project will provide one.

Abstract

Florida generates millions of tons of construction and demolition (C&D) debris annually, yet recycling performance varies widely across counties and is often limited by economic and infrastructure constraints. Despite documented economic and environmental benefits of C&D recycling, most Florida counties operate without a diversion ordinance. County officials seeking to improve C&D recycling rates face a fundamental problem: they lack the analytical tools to evaluate how alternative policy designs, diversion mandates, deposit-refund systems, differential tipping fees, facility certification standards, procurement mandates for recycled materials, or combinations thereof, would perform under their specific local conditions before committing public resources and political capital to implementation.

This project develops a decision-support simulation prototype designed to give county solid waste managers, planners, elected officials, and recycling industry stakeholders a practical means of testing C&D recycling policy options before adopting them. The prototype models the key actors in the C&D waste system (i.e., contractors of different firm sizes making material-by-material disposal decisions, recycling facilities with specific processing capacities and gate policies, landfills, end-product markets, and regulatory bodies) and captures how these actors interact and respond when policy conditions change. The model accounts for the factors that drive disposal decisions in practice such as cost, convenience, compliance pressure, risk tolerance, and the reality that a large demolition firm with established recycling relationships will respond to a new ordinance very differently than a small contractor accustomed to landfilling.

The research is carried out in four phases. The first documents the current regulatory environment, fee structures, facility characteristics, and contractor practices across Florida counties. The second translates these findings into a structured model of how stakeholders make decisions, how materials flow through the system, and where policy interventions take effect. The third builds and validates the simulation, calibrating it against observed diversion rates in a representative Florida county with different policy environments and facility infrastructure. The fourth applies the validated prototype to test various policy architectures. Each scenario is tested under county-specific conditions to determine not only whether a policy improves diversion, but by how much, at what cost, with what impact on different sizes of contracting firms, and with what unintended consequences including illegal dumping, processing bottlenecks at recycling facilities, material leakage to neighboring counties, and market saturation of recycled products.

This prototype is designed from the outset to demonstrate a practitioner-operated tool, developed and tested for a single Florida county as a proof of concept. County staff can input their own local parameters (e.g., tipping fees, facility locations, contractor populations, hauling distances, and current diversion rates) and receive outputs that directly inform policy decisions: projected diversion rates with confidence intervals, compliance costs broken down by contractor firm size, facility utilization and bottleneck risk, enforcement staffing requirements, net fiscal impact to the county, and environmental benefits including estimated greenhouse gas reductions.

Research approach

Four phases take the project from what is happening in Florida counties today to tested policy options.

1

Definition: building the knowledge base

Document regulations, fee structures, and facilities across Florida counties. Validate behavioral assumptions through interviews with contractors, facility operators, and county solid waste managers. Map material flows and facilities within a 30-mile haul radius.

2

Abstraction: conceptual model

Translate the findings into a structured model of who makes which decisions, how materials move through the system, and where policies take effect.

3

Implementation and validation

Build the simulation, then validate it in three stages: review of the model logic by the TAG, calibration against a representative Florida county, and sensitivity analysis.

4

Scenario analysis

Test policy options under county-specific conditions: how much diversion each achieves, at what cost, for which contractors, and with what side effects.

Side effects the model tracks

Policies produce effects that no single actor sees on their own. The model flags them before a policy is adopted, not after.

Illegal dumping

When legal disposal becomes more expensive

Facility bottlenecks

When redirected material exceeds processing capacity

Cross-county leakage

When loads move to cheaper facilities next door

Market saturation

When recycled supply outpaces demand

Burden on small contractors

Reported by contractor firm size

Who benefits and how

The prototype is built for the people who make, carry out, and live with C&D recycling policy in Florida. Each group gets answers it cannot get today.

County solid waste directors and planners

County-specific answers before a policy goes to the commission.

When staff recommend a diversion ordinance, commissioners ask predictable questions. Will it work here, or only in counties like Lee? Can our recycling facilities handle the extra material? Will contractors comply, pay the fee, or dump illegally? What will it cost to administer? Will it hurt small contractors? The prototype answers these with numbers for the county’s own conditions:

  • Whether a diversion target is achievable with the county’s facility capacity, contractor base, and haul distances, and the point where processing bottlenecks begin
  • The probability of meeting the target, not just the average projected rate, so officials see the downside risk
  • Net fiscal impact: revenue from diversion fees, deposits, or surcharges against the cost of enforcement
  • Cost per additional ton diverted, for side-by-side comparison of policy options
  • Compliance costs by contractor firm size
  • Tons diverted from landfill and greenhouse gas emissions avoided, ready for commission presentations, public comment responses, and grant applications

FDEP and state policymakers

Evidence for statewide guidance.

The platform addresses a longstanding question: should Florida develop a statewide model C&D diversion ordinance, and if so, how should it be structured?

  • In a follow-on phase, the same ordinance design can be run across county templates (urban and rural, high and low construction activity, infrastructure-rich and infrastructure-poor) to show which elements to standardize statewide and which to leave to local discretion
  • Evaluates state-level levers outside county ordinances: FDOT specification approval for recycled C&D aggregate in state road construction, state grants for recycling facility equipment upgrades, and possible statewide material-specific disposal restrictions

Demolition contractors and industry associations

Compliance cost data for public comment.

When an ordinance is under public review, contractors and their associations need numbers, not assertions.

  • Compliance cost impact by firm size for each policy option
  • Policies where compliance costs less than non-compliance: incentive-aligned options contractors have reason to support
  • The project types, material streams, and locations where a policy creates the most difficulty
  • Evidence for recommending exemption thresholds, phase-in schedules, and accommodations for small contractors

C&D recycling facility operators

Forward-looking volumes for investment planning.

Operators decide whether to expand capacity, buy new sorting or crushing equipment, or seek certification based on what they expect to receive.

  • Projected inbound volumes, material composition, and contamination levels under each policy option
  • Which quality and performance standards would give the greatest competitive advantage under different policy configurations, so operators can prioritize improvements
